# Qualified writing

For every piece of prose a session writes: documents, commit
messages, pull request bodies, tracker tasks, and chat replies alike. Local
`CLAUDE.md` rules override these defaults.

---

Qualified writing is clear and precise. No em dashes outside quotations: use colons, commas, semicolons, parentheses, or new sentences.

## Perspective

1. **Reference.** Introduce before you refer. For any pronoun or definite phrase, the immediate context must identify a single referent.
   *Not:* This exacerbated the problem. *But:* The delayed handoff increased the reporting errors.
2. **Terms.** Use standard terms for a general audience. Do not adopt shorthand lingo on seeing it used, but address it and question it.
   *Not:* The log keeps two properties. *But:* /incidents.csv has two columns.
3. **Flow.** Extend from what has been established. Develop the current point, or clearly name the earlier point you are resuming.
   *Not:* The solution is to increase funding. *But:* Since the $2M budget gap cannot be closed by contract renegotiation, we turn to increased funding.

## Qualifiers

4. **Qualify noun phrases.** Attach the words that narrow what a claim means to address. Avoid a bare *this* or *it*, lazy and informal abstractions like "the thing", and bare quantifiers as in "some say…".
   *Not:* Both are good. *But:* Both restructuring proposals are good.
   *Not:* The figures in italics, which should be read as projections rather than booked amounts, total $4M. *But:* The projected figures total $4M.

## Accuracy

5. **Scope.** Consider claiming less. Overstatement raises qualms and shifts attention from the argument. A narrower claim often clarifies and solidifies the whole. Use stronger claims when the conclusion rests on prevalence. In every case, stay within the evidence.
6. **Verdicts.** Report what a source says and what was observed, not a verdict on either. Words such as "forbids", "requires", "violates", "proves" and "confirms" claim more than a quote or a measurement shows. When you restate a source, keep its qualifiers, and mark your own inferences and groupings as yours.
   *Not:* The test proves the fix works. *But:* The test passes after the fix.
   *Not:* The owner decided to drop the tab. *But:* The owner said the tab "might not be needed".

## Instructions

7. **Conditions and actions.** Write a rule as a condition and an action, with the actor as the subject. State the condition by naming the cases that meet it, and use verbs the actor performs.
   *Not:* A wake that leaves the reader nothing new gets no reply. *But:* Reply to a wake when it brings a merge, a failing check or a review comment.
   *Not:* A kit that wants Alpine reactivity gets a component wrapper. *But:* When a kit needs Alpine reactivity, wrap it in a component.
