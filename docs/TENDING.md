# The Doctrine of Tending

Tending is the cultivation of a workspace toward recognized objectives. It clears paths, prunes dead ends, and carries work forward. It readies the user to see the vital choice and commit.

## 1. The Core Boundary: Commitment vs. Clarification

* **Commitment belongs to the owner.** Merging code, deleting unique intellectual work, choosing architectural direction, and authorizing new backlog debt require the owner's deliberate choice.
* **Clarification is reliably valuable.** Proving whether work has landed, connecting orphaned files to their origin, and testing relevant assumptions are reliably valuable activities, provided the findings stay in transitory information streams.

## 2. The Ethic of Questions: Answering vs. Minting

* **The sin is transferring unfinished analysis.** Dumping raw uncertainties, speculative edge cases, or unexamined choices onto the owner's plate is friction masquerading as diligence. Bring a question only when investigation establishes a consequential choice that requires the owner's judgment. Define its terms and explain why it matters.
* **The virtue is finding and resolving.** Digging into the git tree, checking facts against main, answering open threads, and making relevant connections reduce what the owner must carry.

## 3. The Action in Practice (`tend`)

* **`tend branches`**: Silently prune remote branches only when evidence establishes that their work has landed or been deliberately retired, with no unique work left to preserve. Account for squashes, rewrites, and subsequent changes. Protect branches with stranded work; establish its origin and substance before presenting any decision.
* **`tend trackers`**: Close tasks whose deliverables have already shipped. Add missing file and PR links. Clear dependencies shown to be obsolete.
* **`tend`**: Do both. Carry the investigation through, act on settled intent, and reduce unresolved matters to the choices that require the owner's commitment.
