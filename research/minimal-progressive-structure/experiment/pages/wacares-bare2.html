Washington ballot measure · Senate Joint Resolution 8201

Voters to decide if WA Cares tax dollars can be invested in stock market

A proposed constitutional amendment would let assets be managed like the state’s pension funds. A similar attempt failed in 2020.

Based on reporting by Jerry Cornfield, Washington State Standard, published Friday, October 31, 2025. Relative dates below (“Tuesday,” “this year”) are as of that date.

The question on the ballot

The first item on every Washington voter’s ballot is a proposal to add a few lines to the state constitution that would change how billions of taxpayer dollars are managed.

What it does
Lets the state make stock market investments with the payroll taxes collected for WA Cares, the state’s long-term care program.
Who would invest
The Washington State Investment Board, which could handle the program’s assets in a similar manner as it does pension and retirement funds.
Where earnings go
Any earnings would have to be used solely for the program’s beneficiaries.
What it takes to pass
It becomes law if more than 50% of voters approve it on Tuesday.

What would change

The state constitution generally bars investing public money in the stock of private companies. The amendment would add the WA Cares account to the list of funds exempt from that rule.

Today

Bonds and other fixed-income only

  • State and local governments are limited to fixed-income securities like government bonds, treasury bills and certificates of deposit.
  • These are less risky but typically deliver lower rates of return.
  • The WA Cares account is now subject to this limitation.
If approved

Managed like the pension funds

  • The long-term service account would join the list of funds exempt from the prohibition, alongside the state’s pension and retirement funds.
  • The State Investment Board could then decide if it wants to invest in private equity funds and buy stock of private companies, or not.
  • Any earnings would have to be used solely for the program’s beneficiaries.

For and against

The two sides made their cases in a recent debate at the University of Washington.

For

Heather Weiner

Approve 8201 campaign

  • The money would be invested in a safe and risk averse way while producing greater returns for the program.
  • Since 1992, annual returns for Washington’s pension funds have averaged 8.9%, several percentage points higher than investments in government bonds. In the long run, that would mean a lot more dollars for the program.
  • Unlike the 2020 proposal, this one mandates that investment earnings go into the WA Cares Fund. The intent is to prevent lawmakers from tapping those dollars for other budget needs.
This doesn’t mean day trading, taking big risks on a stock market. This means smart long-term [investing] so that in 50 years when people in this audience today will need long term care, that fund will be there for you.

Backed by

  • Democratic Gov. Bob Ferguson
  • Senate Minority Leader John Braun, R-Centralia
  • Washington State Nurses Association
  • Washington State Council of Fire Fighters
  • AARP
  • Washington Health Care Association

Campaign money

Service Employees International Union 775 is underwriting the campaign. The union, which represents 55,000 long-term care workers in Washington, Montana and Alaska, had reported raising and spending $2.5 million as of Friday: five times more than it spent in a losing effort in 2020.

Against

State Sen. Bob Hasegawa

D-Tukwila

  • The state’s fiduciary responsibility is to the people, and no one can know when the economy or the stock market will crash.
  • The vote is about keeping prudent fiscal management of tax dollars and retaining the constitution’s protections.
Our fiduciary responsibility is to the people. No one is going to know when the economy or stock market crashes. So, this vote is about whether we keep prudent fiscal management policies of our tax dollars and retain those constitutional protections.

Voters’ pamphlet

Hasegawa and three Republican state lawmakers wrote the voters’ pamphlet statement opposing the measure.

Campaign money

Hasegawa said there is no funded opposition campaign, just as in the last ballot fight.

About WA Cares

WA Cares is Washington’s long-term care program. The amendment is about how its payroll tax dollars may be invested.

Created by
A 2019 state law.
Paid for by
A 0.58% tax on the paychecks of most workers in Washington. Collections began in mid-2023.
Money saved so far
More than $2.5 billion was banked in the Long Term Services and Supports Trust Account as of summer.
The benefit
A lifetime amount of $36,500, set to rise in future years to account for inflation.
When it starts
Those who qualify can begin accessing the benefit in July 2026. A pilot program will start in January in a handful of counties.

How we got here

Voters turned down a similar measure in 2020.

  1. 2019

    A state law creates WA Cares.

  2. 2020

    Voters defeat a similar measure. It passed in only three Washington counties: King, Jefferson and Whatcom. Weiner said this year’s proposal, unlike that one, mandates that money made from investments go into the WA Cares Fund.

  3. Mid-2023

    Collection of the 0.58% payroll tax begins.

  4. Earlier this year

    The Legislature votes to put the amendment on the ballot: 86-9 in the House and 42-7 in the Senate.

  5. This summer

    More than $2.5 billion has been banked in the Long Term Services and Supports Trust Account.

  6. Tuesday

    Voters decide. The amendment becomes law if more than 50% approve.

  7. January

    A WA Cares pilot program starts in a handful of counties.

  8. July 2026

    Those who qualify can begin accessing the $36,500 lifetime benefit.
