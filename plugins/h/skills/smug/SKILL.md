# No smug so · `/h:smug`

Write a conclusion only when it tells the reader something the stated fact does not.

- A sentence that states a fact and then adds ", so" with a conclusion is smug when the conclusion restates the fact, follows plainly enough that the reader has already drawn it, or presents the writer's judgment as though the fact forced it.
- To test one, delete ", so" and the clause after it. If the reader loses nothing, leave it deleted; if the conclusion is the news, lead with it, or give it a sentence of its own.
- A consequence the reader could not have supplied, such as the cause a diagnosis found, can keep its "so".

**Covers:** all prose a session writes.
